Concept

An original angel-of-death valkyrie: elegant mechanical wings and a dark halo-like crown frame a poised warrior carrying a great scythe. Mercy is one concept inspiration for the winged sci-fi valkyrie contrast, not the character being depicted. The primary composition is the poised reaper stance described below; more kinetic sweep, pirouette, and aerial treatments remain proposed alternatives. Mantlepiece display piece, ~10–15 cm tall (final height capped by Ken's printer).

Identity carriers

The features that establish her as her own character: a dark asymmetrical mechanical wing array, a halo-like death crown, the poised S-curve stance, the great scythe, and a new sculpted armor language. Hair, face, armor panels, and headpiece must together establish an identity distinct from Mercy and every other source character behind the design direction.

Body and face

  • Proportions: slender adult feminine build, realistic display-statue proportions (not heroic-miniature exaggeration; authored inference from the proportion direction, pending confirmation at v1).
  • Face: mature East Asian features, calm direct gaze, softly angular oval face. An original face — not any celebrity's likeness.
  • Hair: long and elegant (authored inference from the pose direction, pending confirmation at v1); it may hang with a gentle echo of the wing arc but must not stream as if mid-attack (see Pose). Exact style is open until v1.

Wings

A dark mechanical wing array: layered solid metal wing-blades with silver structural framing and red accents. Both wings attach through explicit, solid roots at the upper back — never detached or floating. The array is deliberately asymmetrical in presentation (one wing mass carried high, the other sweeping low; the Pose section pins the sides). Wing glow, if any, is a paint effect, not geometry.

Scythe

The only prop, and the design's twist: an elegant valkyrie weapon in her suit's design language, not a farm tool. Its pose gets pinned geometrically in every view — a two-handed pole plus a broad blade is maximal ghost-prop risk.

  • Haft: a continuous armored haft — thick, sculpted, no slender pole — with a spear-like counterweight projecting beyond the upper grip.
  • Blade: an oversized, broad, solid crescent with an ornate mechanical head where it meets the haft; techno-gothic in character. No thin spikes, cut-outs, or fragile openwork; simplified ornament that survives reconstruction and printing.
  • Hold: two-handed, with the two grips widely separated on the haft. Both hands must be unmistakable five-fingered wraps around a clean haft, with visible thumbs and unbroken shoulder-to-hand anatomy. At least one concept/turnaround view must prove each grip.

Armor language (original design)

A new sleek, solid death-valkyrie suit that unifies the dark wing architecture, the readable scythe, and the display-scale silhouette. No source outfit is being reconstructed. Panel lines and details sized to survive at display scale; footwear reads as printable armored contacts rather than stiletto points.

Halo / death crown (original design)

A distinct halo-like death crown that reads ceremonially from both front and side. It must not reproduce Mercy's headpiece, any franchise silhouette, or logo geometry.

Palette and motif

Silver/black/red hierarchy: dark gunmetal/black primary surfaces, silver structural framing and edge highlights, red accents. The styling motif is skull/death-themed — ominous, elegant, ceremonial — expressed through sculpted forms and paint-stage detail rather than literal copied skull ornaments. (Colour applies to the photoreal concept round and the eventual paint plan; the turnaround and mesh stages are monochrome clay per the pipeline.)

Optional hood (undecided)

A long, deep, face-framing hood with a short shoulder mantle is under consideration only. Do not include it in a design unless the round's runbook selects it; do not treat it as chosen until Jake or Dimitri resolves it.

Design rules (pipeline-facing)

  • Everything solid and opaque. Wing glow and any scythe energy are paint effects — translucent geometry produces real mesh defects.
  • Stylize thick: armored haft (no slender pole), broad solid crescent blade, wings as solid blades, unsupported points kept short — for both reconstruction and shelf life.
  • Display scale raises the mesh bar. Meshy detail is figure-relative; roughness invisible at 32 mm shows at 120 mm. Face/hands get explicit review gates; budget for a refinement round.

Pose direction

All left/right below is the viewer's left/right in the named view, per the pipeline's global chirality policy. The full per-view L/R anchor program gets written at v1 pinning; the scythe (a two-handed asymmetric prop) is the highest-risk mirroring case, so those anchors are not optional.

Primary — poised crescent

The primary composition reads as poised, statuesque, and ominous — an elegant, controlled character portrait, not a mid-swing action frame. Do not add swing-speed cues that fight it: hair and cloth can hang with a gentle echo of the wing arc rather than streaming as if she were mid-attack.

  • Body line and balance: a tall, near-frontal three-quarter contrapposto with a pronounced vertical S-curve. The pelvis sits under a lifted, backward-arched chest; the head tilts back and sideways while turning toward the viewer. In the front three-quarter view, the viewer-right leg is almost straight and carries the weight; the viewer-left leg is relaxed and slightly bent, its pointed foot visibly lifted. Keep that long, graceful line and one-leg-led balance rather than converting it into a low combat lunge.
  • Arms and two-handed hold: two widely spaced grips on the diagonal haft — an upper hand around waist level on the viewer's left, and a lower hand beside the upper thigh on the viewer's right. Both arms are relaxed; the hold is low-key, not straining. Both grips follow the hand-proof rule in the Scythe section.
  • Scythe line: the haft cuts steeply from upper viewer-left to lower viewer-right, passing behind or immediately alongside the body — never through the torso. The counterweight projects beyond the upper grip; the ornate blade head sits low on the viewer's right; and the oversized crescent sweeps close to ground level behind the calves and across toward the lower viewer-left. This strong descending diagonal and low crescent are the main compositional anchors. The blade must clear the legs in a provable plane, and any blade-to-base contact must be designed rather than accidental.
  • Wing counter-sweep: one large wing mass rises toward the upper viewer-right and the other sweeps down and out toward the lower viewer-left, opposing the haft diagonal and creating the composition's swirl even though the body, hair, and garment are mostly still. Both wings keep their explicit, solid roots at the back.
  • Support and contacts: the pose has only one weight-bearing foot, so it requires a deliberate load-bearing solution: a thick central/scenic support rising from the base, hidden or integrated behind the body, cloth, or wing sweep. A concept may instead lower the relaxed foot into a real second ground contact if it preserves the same S-curve. Footwear is broadened into printable armored contacts; no narrow high-heel point carries load.

Proposed alternatives

  • A. Grounded sweep — both feet at ground level, low lunge, scythe mid-arc, wings flared. Safest, and the only option that satisfies the pipeline's basing rule as-is.
  • B. Pirouette swirl — one planted foot, body actively spiraling; trailing cloth/wing sweep connects to the base as the second contact. Medium risk and more kinetic than the primary.
  • C. Aerial reap — airborne, carried entirely by the mounting structure. Most dramatic and hardest for reconstruction.

Basing-rule note: the primary pose as described, B, and C deviate from the pipeline skill's both-feet-at-ground-level rule (Jason, 2026-07-10) — a lifted foot can't be glued to the base and forces pinning/mounting at assembly. Dimitri approved exploring aerial/ungrounded poses for this one-off display piece (2026-08-03), on the condition that the design includes a real mounting mechanism — e.g. a central support pillar rising from the base — and, if we get fancy, the pillar hidden behind billowing cloth or the wing/cloth swirl. Any r1 runbook whose matrix includes the ungrounded primary treatment, B, or C restates this recorded exception.

Explicitly absent

  • NO depiction or exact likeness of Mercy, Lucy Liu, Aerith, or any other source character behind the design direction.
  • NO copied franchise costume, halo, wing assembly, hood outfit, or signature prop; NO Caduceus staff and NO blaster — the scythe is the only prop.
  • NO transparent/glowing sculpt elements; glow is paint-stage.
  • NO text, logos, game marks, or branded base details on figure or base.
