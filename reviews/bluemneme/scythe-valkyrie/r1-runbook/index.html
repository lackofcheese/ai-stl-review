Goal

Jake is happy with the Scythe Valkyrie concept; what's undecided is a small set of design questions the spec lists as open decisions — above all the hood, and how a hood coexists with her death halo/crown. Rather than argue those in the abstract, this round renders three complete character directions across five decision-carrying image models so Jake and Dimitri can map the design space by eye: every image is the same valkyrie, same pose, same palette — only the hood/halo treatment and the support/base treatment change. The winning answers feed straight into pinning spec v1. (Two additional trial models render each direction as admission evidence for the model policy; those images never carry the design decision.)

The choices on the table

Five decision-carrying models each render all three directions: the four ratified fresh-concept defaults — flash31, gptimage2, seedream45, grokq — plus riverflow25 as a named Supported specialist (proven text-to-image concept quality: it produced Gorgon v1's r4 gold concept, and this display piece sets a higher rendering bar than a gaming mini). Every identity, wing, scythe, pose, and palette sentence is held byte-identical across the three prompts; only the two named variant paragraphs — HOOD AND HALO, and SUPPORT AND BASE — differ.

Alongside them, two named trial arms run the same three prompts (Dimitri, 2026-08-04, explicitly widening this round beyond the one-arm default): mai25pro (MAI-Image-2.5-Pro — the policy's registered trial #5 and the only currently routable model offering a genuinely new attractor; moderation reliability and chirality tracked separately per its trial notes) and flashlite31 (Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite Image, 1K — its second distinct admission context after Tiger r1, which is exactly what promotion requires). Trial outputs are admission evidence for the model policy and do NOT carry the hood/halo or support/base decision.

Each direction deliberately bundles one hood/halo answer with one support/base answer so fifteen images cover the whole map (Dimitri asked for variety over a controlled single-variable test). The bundles stay readable because the two axes live in different parts of the frame: judge the hood question on the head and shoulders, and the mounting/base question on the legs, blade, and ground — mix-and-match verdicts ("V2's hood with V1's base") are explicitly welcome.

No images are attached. Create a brand-new text-to-image concept from this description alone.

OUTPUT AND CAMERA
A photorealistic full-body studio character render designed as production concept art for a premium resin DISPLAY STATUE, roughly 12-15 cm tall, of an original character. Show ONE figure only, in a NEAR-FRONTAL THREE-QUARTER view, centered on a plain neutral dark-grey studio background with even, softly dramatic lighting. Frame the ENTIRE composition with clear space around every silhouette edge: the whole body, the base she stands on, the full spread of BOTH wings, and the complete scythe from counterweight to blade tip. This is character concept art, not a photograph of a toy: no presentation card, no watermark, and no text, labels, logos, or game marks anywhere in the image.

HOOD AND HALO — V1: NO HOOD, CROWN FULLY REVEALED
She wears NO hood and no mantle: her hair and face are fully revealed. Rising above and behind her head is her DARK DEATH CROWN — an ominous halo-like crown structure in the suit's own design language: dark gunmetal with silver edge highlights, ceremonial and ominous, reading clearly from both front and side. It is mounted solidly to her head or the back of her armor through visible sculpted supports — a thick, printable structure, not a floating wire, and not any existing franchise halo design.

HELD-CONSTANT CHARACTER PROGRAM
She is an original ANGEL-OF-DEATH VALKYRIE — an elegant, ominous, ceremonial reaper, played absolutely straight. Slender adult feminine build with realistic display-statue proportions, not heroic-miniature exaggeration. Her face has mature East Asian features, a calm direct gaze, and a softly angular oval face — an original face, no real person's likeness. Her long dark hair is elegant and sculptural; it may hang with a gentle echo of the wing arc, but it must NOT stream as if she were mid-attack.

Her suit is a sleek, solid death-valkyrie armor in an original design language: dark gunmetal and black primary surfaces, silver structural framing and edge highlights, restrained deep-red accents. Panel lines are bold and sculptural, sized to survive at display scale. The styling motif is skull-and-death themed — ominous, elegant, ceremonial — expressed through the sculpted forms themselves, never through printed graphics. Her footwear reads as armored contacts, not stiletto points.

Her WINGS are a dark MECHANICAL wing array: layered SOLID metal wing-blades with silver structural framing and red accents. Both wings attach through explicit, solid mechanical roots at her upper back — never detached, never floating. One wing mass is carried high and the other sweeps low; the pose paragraph pins the sides.

Her ONLY prop is a GREAT SCYTHE in the same design language — an elegant valkyrie weapon, not a farm tool. The haft is a continuous ARMORED haft, thick and sculpted, never a slender pole, with a spear-like counterweight projecting beyond the upper grip. The blade is an oversized, broad, SOLID crescent joined to the haft by an ornate mechanical head — techno-gothic, with no thin spikes, no cut-outs, no fragile openwork. She holds it two-handed with the grips widely separated: BOTH hands are unmistakable five-fingered wraps around the clean haft, thumbs visible, with unbroken shoulder-to-hand anatomy. She carries nothing else — no firearm, no staff, no second prop.

POSE AND COMPOSITION
The pose is POISED, STATUESQUE, and OMINOUS — a controlled character portrait, never a mid-swing action frame. She stands in a tall near-frontal three-quarter contrapposto with a pronounced vertical S-curve: the pelvis sits under a lifted, backward-arched chest, and her head tilts back and to the side while her eyes turn toward the viewer. The VIEWER'S RIGHT leg is nearly straight and carries her weight; the VIEWER'S LEFT leg is relaxed and slightly bent. Her upper hand grips the haft around waist level on the VIEWER'S LEFT; her lower hand grips beside her upper thigh on the VIEWER'S RIGHT. The haft cuts steeply from upper VIEWER'S LEFT to lower VIEWER'S RIGHT, passing behind or immediately alongside her body — never through her torso. The ornate blade head sits low on the VIEWER'S RIGHT, and the oversized crescent sweeps close to ground level BEHIND her calves, reaching across toward the lower VIEWER'S LEFT with clearly visible clearance from her legs. One large wing mass rises toward the upper VIEWER'S RIGHT; the other sweeps down and out toward the lower VIEWER'S LEFT, opposing the haft diagonal and swirling the composition while the body itself stays still.

SUPPORT AND BASE — V1: LIFTED FOOT, HIDDEN SCENIC SUPPORT, SCENIC BASE
Her relaxed VIEWER'S-LEFT foot is visibly LIFTED off the ground, its armored toe pointed and free, while all her weight flows through the straight VIEWER'S-RIGHT leg. A SOLID sculpted support column rises from the base behind her lower body and carries the real load, but it is mostly CONCEALED behind the low-sweeping wing and a heavy fall of dark cloth, so at a glance she appears to barely touch the ground. The base is a SCENIC round display base: dark shattered ground and broken battlefield stone in the same dark palette. The crescent blade stays clearly CLEAR of the base — visible daylight between blade and ground.

PRINT-SAFE FINISH
Treat every feature as geometry for a resin display statue roughly 12-15 cm tall: bold planes, thick connected forms, deep relief. Every material is OPAQUE and SOLID — no glass, no transparency, no translucent glowing parts; any glow is at most a painted surface effect on solid geometry. No hairline detail, no thin spike, no free-floating strap or ribbon. The scythe blade and every wing-blade are thick solid plates; hair masses are sculptural, thick, and connected to the figure. The halo/crown structure is thick enough to print and solidly mounted, never a floating wire.

How the round is judged

Jake's preferences steer, Dimitri decides (art gate). Three reading notes:

  • Compare directions within each model first — the seven models draw faces, cloth, and metal very differently, and comparing V1-from-one-model against V2-from-another compares house styles, not directions.
  • The hood axis (none / up / down) is judged on the upper third of each image; the support/base axis (hidden pillar / second foot contact / crescent-into-base, scenic / plinth) on the lower third. Split verdicts across axes are expected and useful.
  • The halo/crown is an original-design part with deliberate freedom left in the prompt — variety in how models invent it is data for the v1 spec, not a defect (only a franchise-copied or unprintable halo is a failure).

Machine checks run in two explicit lanes:

  • FAST: a fresh-context crop audit runs as each image lands, checking the held-constant program — both five-fingered grips with visible thumbs on a clean armored haft, solid crescent with no cut-outs or thin spikes, both wings rooted at the upper back, viewer-frame composition (haft upper-left to lower-right, high wing viewer-right, low wing viewer-left, crescent behind the calves with visible leg clearance), poised S-curve rather than a combat lunge, opaque solid materials with no translucent glow geometry, no text or logos, no franchise likeness (in particular nothing reading as Mercy or any named source character), and the direction's own hood/halo and support/base paragraph. A mirrored composition is recorded as a warn, not a reject: at concept stage a deterministic flip restores chirality, and the design decision survives the mirror. Once all FAST verdicts are logged, a private review may be shared as PROVISIONAL — slow checks pending so eye-review can start.
  • SLOW: after all 21 images land, one blind_detect_round.py manifest submits every spec-blind description in a single Gemini Batch wave and every dependent comparison in a second Batch wave. Completed records append to reviews.jsonl; every blind-vs-crop disagreement gets a mandatory human look. Dimitri then appends one authoritative verdict per artifact, after both machine records, whose summary starts RECONCILED — AGREE: when the screens agree or RECONCILED — RESOLVED: with the human resolution when they do not. The panel is rebuilt as COMPLETE only after the canonical review log contains crop audits, successful records from that exact Batch run, and later reconciliation verdicts for all and only the 21 planned artifact ids. The provisional panel cannot close the round, promote a keeper, or authorize downstream work.

The six trial renders get the identical screening but are judged separately on their own trial questions, and never count toward the direction pick: for mai25pro, does the new attractor deliver usable geometry/pose quality — with Azure output-moderation reliability and facing/chirality each recorded explicitly, per its trial notes; for flashlite31, does a 1K Flash Lite render hold silhouette, pose, and the held-constant program well enough for cheap selection bake-offs (its second admission context after Tiger r1)?

Basing-rule exception (restated as the spec requires): V1 and V3 keep the primary pose's lifted foot, deviating from the pipeline's both-feet-at-ground-level rule (Jason, 2026-07-10). Dimitri approved exploring aerial/ungrounded poses for this one-off display piece (2026-08-03) on the condition that the design includes a real mounting mechanism — V1's hidden support column and V3's designed blade-to-base contact are exactly that mechanism, and rendering them is part of what this round evaluates.

# Coordinator completion is not sufficient: PARTIAL is a durable terminal
# state too. After the blind run lands, Dimitri appends one authoritative
# judgment record per artifact through review_log.py. Its final KEEPER/FIXABLE/
# REJECT call is the panel verdict, and its summary must begin with exactly one
# of these evidence markers:
#   RECONCILED — AGREE: <why the crop and blind judgment classes agree>
#   RECONCILED — RESOLVED: <what disagreed and Dimitri's resolution>
# Append that record only after reading both source records. The check below
# requires all and only the expected 21 crop audits, same-run blind records,
# and later human reconciliations before COMPLETE/public handoff. It derives
# each latest machine call's KEEPER/FIXABLE/REJECT class and rejects AGREE when
# those classes differ.
jq -e -s --arg run "$DETECT_RUN" --rawfile expected "$EXPECTED" '
  def call_class:
    (. // "" | match("^(KEEPER|FIXABLE|REJECT)")?.string);
  ($expected | split("\n") | map(select(length > 0)) | sort) as $want
  | to_entries as $rows
  | ([$rows[]
      | select(.value.reviewer | endswith("-crop-audit"))]) as $crops
  | ([$rows[]
      | select(.value.reviewer == "blind-detect"
               and .value.batch_run_id == $run)]) as $blinds
  | ([$rows[]
      | select(.value.reviewer == "dimitri"
               and ((.value.summary // "")
                    | test("^RECONCILED — (AGREE|RESOLVED): .+")))]) as $human
  | ([$crops[].value.artifact] | unique | sort) as $crop_ids
  | ([$blinds[].value.artifact] | unique | sort) as $blind_ids
  | ([$human[].value.artifact] | unique | sort) as $human_ids
  | ($want | length) == 21
    and $crop_ids == $want
    and $blind_ids == $want
    and $human_ids == $want
    and all($human[];
      (.value.call // "") | test("^(KEEPER|FIXABLE|REJECT)"))
    and all($want[]; . as $id |
      ([$crops[] | select(.value.artifact == $id)] | max_by(.key)) as $crop
      | ([$blinds[] | select(.value.artifact == $id)] | max_by(.key)) as $blind
      | ([$human[] | select(.value.artifact == $id)] | max_by(.key)) as $recon
      | ($crop.value.call | call_class) as $crop_class
      | ($blind.value.call | call_class) as $blind_class
      | $recon.key > $crop.key
        and $recon.key > $blind.key
        and (if $crop_class == $blind_class
             then ($recon.value.summary | startswith("RECONCILED — AGREE:"))
             else ($recon.value.summary | startswith("RECONCILED — RESOLVED:"))
             end))
' "$T/reviews.jsonl" > /dev/null
