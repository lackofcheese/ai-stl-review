Prior round
r14's closing feedback (final, 2026-07-12) sent us to re-crop the TreeMolin source properly. The first attempt at that was rejected on 2026-08-02: parts of the sculpt cut out including holes in the hands, backdrop still showing in the gaps between the arms and legs, and the base cut shearing the feet. Dimitri asked for two crop approaches — a simpler rectangular one and a second isolation attempt — and, once the crop was fixed, for the cleaned turnaround to be fed straight into Meshy as a worthwhile experiment. This round is that experiment, on the rectangular crop.

Goal

Two things. (1) Settle the reference: cut the four sculpt photos as identically-sized rectangles anchored on each display base, with the backdrop and base left exactly as photographed. (2) Test the shortcut. Every round since r11 has assumed the path to an STL runs through a generated clay turnaround — photos in, clay sheet out, clay sheet into Meshy. This round asks whether that middle step is needed at all by feeding the photographic turnaround directly to Meshy. If the reconstruction is good, a whole class of generation rounds stops being necessary; if it breaks, we know what the clay conversion is actually buying us and r16 proceeds with that settled.

The choices on the table

The crop is not a choice any more — the rectangular approach is what this round runs, and the second isolation attempt is parked (see below). The choice is what we learn from two Meshy rolls on it.

Two rolls, not one: feature defects are a per-roll lottery, so the standing rule is two rolls per keeper and pick the defect-free one. Both rolls take identical inputs — the difference between them is Meshy's own stochasticity, which is the thing being measured.

Why the rectangular crop, and what happened to the other one. The rejected crop was an isolating one: mask the figure, paint out everything else, cut the base away. The second attempt fixed two of the three defects — bounded hysteresis stopped the sculpt being bitten, and subtracting the base as a *region* rather than cutting below its far rim stopped the feet being sheared (that was the actual bug: the ellipse's top arc is the base's far rim, and the feet live below it). It still leaked in one panel and left a ragged fringe. More importantly, measuring the backdrop showed why this is hard: it is blue in two panels and chromatically neutral in a third, where the *painted sail* reads bluer than the background behind it. Any rule confident enough to clear the backdrop is confident enough to erase a sail. The rectangular crop has no such rule to get wrong — it changes no pixels at all — so it is what this round uses. The isolation work is preserved on Drive under generated/r15-crop-attempt2/ in case a later round wants it.

How the round is judged

The crop gate comes first and is part of this sign-off. r15 is the first of the two photographic-reference rounds that need a named human to approve the crop evidence at 2× before any paid call — evidence is on this page and in treeman-r15-crop-evidence-{tr,tl,ml,mr}.png, each showing the source box with the rectangle taken, the subject box, and the base anchor, beside the resulting crop. The mechanical checks that back it: the tool refuses outright if the subject falls outside its rectangle, all four crops are byte-identical to the source pixels under them, and any rectangle nudged off its base anchor to stay inside the source box reports the offset (tr and mr are each shifted 11px).

Each returned mesh is then judged as any mesh is: pushed to Drive the moment it lands, validated with trimesh for watertightness and scaled to target height, probed with mesh_thin_parts.py at the tight setting (pitch ~0.28, erode 1) for thin necks around the rotor, and rendered as a turnaround plus zoomed crops of the hands, sails and feet. A fresh-context per-feature review compares each roll against the source photographs as gold, walking a complete feature checklist rather than looking holistically. That review is a pre-filter, not the gate: the gate is Dimitri rotating the real STL in a viewer, at ~50 mm on the longest axis.

The round-specific question on top of that: does this mesh beat what the r14 clay path produced? The r14 diagnostic probe broke at the rotor. If the photographic reconstruction holds the rotor together, the clay sweep planned for r16 is probably unnecessary.

Technical plan — crops, Meshy inputs, commands (pipeline detail)

What changed going in (spec / prompt / process deltas)

  • tools/reference_crops.py gains a rectangle mode. Driven by runbooks/r15-reference-crops.json, which now carries a rectangle block (size: [430, 560], foot_pad: 18) alongside the per-panel source boxes and base ellipses. In this mode the base ellipse is no longer a cut — nothing is cut — it is the *anchor*: the sculpt stands in the middle of its base, so the base's centre and near rim place an identically-sized window the same way in every panel. Because the four frames were shot at one camera distance, identical rectangles preserve the sculpt's true relative scale exactly, where normalising a silhouette bounding box only approximates it (that box breathes with the raised arm). A mask is still computed, but only to check the subject fits; if it does not, the run fails rather than trimming a limb.
  • Panel order is unchanged from r14 and r15's first attempt: TR → TL → ML → MR = −20° → 0° → +45° → 180°, Dimitri's angle read from the physical sculpt in hand. The source's honest weak spot is unchanged and is not fixable by cropping: no true 90° side profile and no rear-three-quarter on either side, so coverage runs −20°→180° with a ~135° gap before the back and nothing on the 180°–340° arc. For a Meshy round this matters more than it did for an image round — the reconstruction will have to invent both sides, and the review should look there first.
  • The PR shield is excluded by source box, not by an exclusion rectangle. It straddles the row gutter and the vertical seam, so it reaches into all four cells; and because it sits on the edge bands the background ramp is estimated from, leaving it inside a box poisons the background model for every row it touches.
  • Meshy inputs are the four panels as-is — no meshy_crops.py pass. That tool's rectangular slot split is for clean generated sheets; these panels are already the individual views.
  • No remesh. --no-remesh as standard, so we see what the reconstruction actually produced.
